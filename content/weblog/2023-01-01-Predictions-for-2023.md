#### Major Crypto Exchanges Continue to Fail

After the spectacular collapse of FTX and BlockFi last year - and
increased problems on Genesis and even possibly issues on Binance with
stopped withdrawals, this might be an easy one to take. But, the way
that I see it there are a couple of things that are going to make life
as an individual exchance increasingly difficult in 2023.

1. Increased pressure from traditional financial institutions such as
   Fidelity and newer ones such as Robin Hood to decrease the spread
   and take a lesser commission.
2. Massive skepticism toward shitcoins. Really, it seems like the only
   thing that differentiates some of these platforms is that they
   support all sorts of shitcoins - but the trading volume on these is
   tiny and they are down significantly more in the current crypto
   crisis.
3. Increased regulatatory pressure. I'm not sure if FTX was actually
   trying to be appropriately regulated, but it's clear that both
   parties are going to be out for blood.
4. Exhaustion over NFTs. Many of the exchanges tried to create NFT
   related offerings, but it's pretty clear the market for JPEGs is
   pretty saturated right now.

My prediction here is that a major exchange - such as Gemini, Binance,
Crypto.com, or Coinbase this year.

Note: I've done well with crypto - but that was more dumb luck. If
you'd still like to keep a small position, I highly recommend moving
everything offline to a hardware wallet such as a [Trezor](https://trezor.io/) or a [Ledger](https://www.ledger.com/).

#### Companies Decrease their Investment in AI and Machine Learning

For the last decade we've seen a number of companies making huge
investments toward AI/ML solutions. These investments have
increasingly come under fire for a couple of different reasons. First,
the massive amount of talent and money necessary to competete in these
spaces. It's at the point where training a large language model costs
millions of dollars, and even training Stable Diffusion costs a couple
of hundred thousand dollars. That's if you can actually hire the right
people to build these models, which is extremely hard.

Second, even if you're not building these models, it's difficult to
extract value from many of these speculative AI/ML investments. This
is something that I've seen myself as I've led organizations building
AI/ML across multiple companies and industries. The reality is that
most companies would be better served with basic backwards looking
analytics, and providing those insights to their customers, than by
trying to build out forward looking predictive models.
